The goal of these notes is to develop Chapter 1 of Weibel without appealing to the Freyd-Mitchell Embedding Theorem, and end by proving the result in as much gorey detail as possible.
We begin with an exercise of a more computational flavor, which does not suffer much from this restriction.

\begin{proposition}[Exc. 1.2.5, Weibel]
      Let $C$ be a bounded complex with acylic rows or acyclic columns. 
      Show that $\Tot(C)$ is acyclic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Without loss of generality, we suppose that $C$ has acyclic rows, i.e. $\ker d^h=\im d^h$.
  It suffices to show that $\im(d^h+d^v)$ satisfies the universal property of $\ker(d^h+d^v)$.
  Now, let $f:E\to \Tot(C)_n$ be a morphism such that
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      E\ar[r,"f"]\ar[rr,bend right=20,"0"']&\Tot (C)_n\ar[r,"d^h+d^v"]&\Tot(C)_{n-1}
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  commutes.
  That is,
  \[
    d^hf+d^vf=0.
  \] 
  $C$ is bounded, so we can write
  \begin{align*}
    \Tot(C)_n =\bigoplus_{p+q=n}C_{pq}
              &=\prod_{p+q=n}C_{pq}
  \end{align*}
  as a product.
  In particular, we can write 
  \[
    f = \sum_{p+q=n}f_{pq}
  \] 
  for morphisms
  \[
    f_{pq}:E\to C_{pq}.
  \] 
  Then, for each $p+q=n$, the following diagram commutes and composes to 0.
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      &C_{pq}\ar[dr,"d^h"]\\
      E\ar[ur,"f_{pq}"]\ar[dr,"f_{p+1,q-1}"']&&C_{p,q-1}\\%^{\oplus 2}\ar[r,"+"]&C_{p,q-1}\\
      &C_{p+1,q-1}\ar[ur,"d^v"']
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  % Then, for each $p+q=n$, we have
  % \[
  %   d^hf_{pq}+d^vf_{p+1,q-1}=0.
  % \] 
  Suppose now that $f_{p+1,q-1}$ factors through $\im(d^h+d^v)$ as so:
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      &C_{p+1,q}\ar[dr,"d^h"]\\
      E\ar[rr,"f_{p+1,q-1}"]\ar[ur,"g_{p+1,q}"]\ar[dr,"g_{p+2,q-1}"']&&\im(d^h+d^v)_{p+1,q-1}\ar[r,tail]&C_{p+1,q-1}\\
      &C_{p+2,q-1}\ar[ur,"d^v"']
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  Then we have that
  \begin{align*}
    0 &= d^hf_{pq}+d^v f_{p+1,q-1} \\
      &= d^hf_{pq}+d^v(d^hg_{p+1,q} + d^vg_{p+2,q-1}) \\
      &= d^hf_{pq}+d^vd^hg_{p+1,q} \\
      &= d^h(f_{pq}-d^vg_{p+1,q}),
  \end{align*}
  whence $f_{pq}-d^vg_{p+1,q}\in \ker d^h = \im d^h$ gives us $g_{p,q+1}:E\to C_{p,q+1}$ such that $f_{pq}$ factors through $\im(d^h+d^v)_{pq}$ as follows:%, with $g_{p+1,q}:E\to C_{p+1,q}$ as before:
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      &C_{p,q+1}\ar[dr,"d^h"]\\
      E\ar[rr,"f_{pq}"]\ar[ur,"g_{p,q+1}"]\ar[dr,"g_{p+1,q}"']&&\im(d^h+d^v)_{pq}\ar[r,tail]&C_{pq}\\
      &C_{p+1,q}\ar[ur,"d^v"']
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  Note that this factorization holds trivially if $C_{pq}=0$.
  Then $C$ bounded supplies us with $q_0$ such that $C_{n-q,q}=0$ for all $q<q_0$, whence induction and the universal property of products give us a morphism
  \[
    g=\sum_{p+q=n+1}g_{pq}:E\to \Tot(C)_{n+1}
  \]
  such that
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      E\ar[dr,"d^hg+d^vg"']\ar[r,"f"]&\Tot(C)_n\ar[r,"d^h+d^v"]&\Tot(C)_{n-1}\\
      &\im(d^h+d^v)\ar[u,tail]
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  commutes.
  The map $d^hg+d^vg$ is unique since $\im(d^h+d^v)\inc \Tot(C)_n$ is a monomorphism, so $\im(d^h+d^v)\cong \ker(d^h+d^v)$ by uniqueness of the universal property of kernels.
  We conclude that $\Tot(C)_n$ is acyclic, as desired.
\end{proof}

Before proceeding with Weibel, we wll first prove a series of basic results on additive and abelian categories from Jiantong Lie's lecture notes.
Some of these lemmas may not prove strictly necessary to finish Weibel section 1.2, but will certainly serve us well in proving one of the most difficult obstacles in our journey: the \textit{Snake Lemma}.
Referring to the Snake Lemma with such reverence may seem comical to those that have only gone through the module theoretic proof.
However, it is not so easy to fell without appealing to the behemoth that is Freyd-Mitchell.

\begin{lemma}[Lemma 1.2.7, Liu]
  Consider a commutative square in an additive category $\A$:
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      A'\ar[r,"\alpha"]\ar[d,"f'"'] & A\ar[d,"f"] \\
      B'\ar[r,"\beta"'] & B
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Suppose the square is cartesian, and that $f$ has a kernel.
      Then $\ker f$ is the kernel of $f'$:
      \[
        \begin{tikzcd}
          &\ker f\ar[dl,dotted,tail,"\exists!i'"']\ar[d,tail,"i"] \\
          A'\ar[r,"\alpha"]\ar[d,"f'"'] \ar[dr,phantom, very near start, "\lrcorner"]& A\ar[d,"f"] \\
          B'\ar[r,"\beta"'] & B
        \end{tikzcd}
      \] 
    \item Suppose the square is cocartesian, and that $f'$ has a cokernel.
      Then $\coker f'$ is the cokernel of $f$:
      \[
        \begin{tikzcd}
          A'\ar[r,"\alpha"]\ar[d,"f'"'] & A\ar[d,"f"] \\
          B'\ar[r,"\beta"']\ar[d,twoheadrightarrow,"p'"'] & B\ar[dl,dotted,twoheadrightarrow,"\exists!p"]\ar[ul,phantom, very near start, "\ulcorner"] \\
          \coker f
        \end{tikzcd}
      \] 
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  By symmetry, it suffices to show that (a).
  First, we show uniqueness and existence of $i'$ via the following diagram:
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      \ker f\ar[ddr,bend right= 20,"0"']\ar[drr,bend left = 20,"i"]\ar[dr,dotted,"\exists!i'" description]&\\
      &A'\ar[dr,phantom, very near start, "\lrcorner"] \ar[r,"\alpha"]\ar[d,"f'"'] & A\ar[d,"f"] \\
      &B'\ar[r,"\beta"'] & B
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  Now, suppose $g':C'\to A'$ has $f'g'=0$.
  Then $\alpha g':C'\to A$ has $f\alpha g' = \beta f'g'=0$, and thus factors through $\ker f$ as so:
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      C' \ar[r,dotted,"\exists!g"]\ar[d,"g'"']&\ker f\ar[dl,"i'"']\ar[d,tail,"i"] \\
      A'\ar[r,"\alpha"]\ar[d,"f'"']\ar[dr,phantom, very near start, "\lrcorner"] & A\ar[d,"f"] \\
      B'\ar[r,"\beta"'] & B
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  We wish to show that $g'=i'g$, and that $g$ is the unique morphism with that property.

  Suppose $g'$ factors through $h:C'\to \ker f$.
  That is, $g'=i'h$.
  Recall that $\alpha i'=i$.
  Then 
  \[
    \alpha g' = \alpha i' h = ih
  \]
  gives us $h=g$ by uniqueness of filling morphisms.
  Recall as well that $f'i=0$, and $\alpha g'= ig$.
  Hence,
  \begin{align*}
    \alpha i'g = ig&=\alpha g', \\
    f'i'g = \,0\, &= f'g'
  \end{align*}
  gives us commutativity of the following diagram:
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      C'\ar[ddr,bend right= 30,"0"']\ar[drr,bend left = 30,"ig"]\ar[dr,shift right,"i'g"']\ar[dr,shift left, "g'"]&\\
      &A'\ar[dr,phantom, very near start, "\lrcorner"] \ar[r,"\alpha"]\ar[d,"f'"'] & A\ar[d,"f"] \\
      &B'\ar[r,"\beta"'] & B
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  It follows that $g'=i'g$ by uniqueness of the filling morphisms.
  We conclude that $i':\ker f\inj A'$ is a kernel of $f'$, as desired.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exc. 1.2.7, Weibel]
  Let $C$ be a complex. %, and define complexes $Z(C)$, $B(C)$, and $H(C)$ by $Z(C)_n:= \ker d_n$, $B(C)
  Show that there are exact sequences of complexes:
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0\ar[r] & Z(C)\ar[r] & C\ar[r,"d"] & B(C)[-1]\ar[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{equation}
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{tikzcd}
      0\ar[r] & H(C)\ar[r,"\phi"] & C/B(C)\ar[r,"d"] & Z(C)[-1]\ar[r,"\psi"] & H(C)[-1]\ar[r] & 0
    \end{tikzcd}
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Regard the differential as a map $d:C\to C[-1]$, so that $Z(C)=\ker d$ and $B(C)= \im d[1]$.
  Then $\im d=(\im d[1])[-1]=B(C)[-1]$ gives us well definition and exactness of (1).

  Consider now (2).
  We can regard $H(C)=Z(C)/B(C)=\cok (B(C)\inc Z(C))$.
  Now, we construct our map $\phi:H(C)\to C/B(C)$ by way of the following diagram:
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      B(C)\ar[r,tail] & Z(C)\ar[r,tail]\ar[d,twoheadrightarrow] & C\ar[d,twoheadrightarrow] \\
                      & H(C) \ar[r, dotted, "\exists!\phi"] & C/B(C)
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  Next, we will show that $d:C/B(C)\to Z(C)[-1]$ is well-defined.
  For this purpose, consider the following diagram:
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      B(C)\ar[r,tail] & C\ar[r,"d"] & C[-1]\ar[r,"d{[-1]}"] & C[-2] \\
                      & C/B(C)\ar[u,twoheadrightarrow] \ar[ur,dotted]\ar[r,dotted,"d"'] & Z(C)[-1]\ar[u,tail]
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  By abuse of notation, we will refer to each of these maps as $d$.
  The map $\psi:Z(C)[-1]\to H(C)[-1]$ is the cokernel of $B(C)[-1]\to Z(C)[-1]$, and thus epic.
  We may now proceed to showing exactness!

  We will show $\phi:H(C)\to C/B(C)$ is a monomorphism by showing it is the kernel of $d:C/B(C)\to Z(C)[-1]$.
  Before doing so, we first note that $\im d=\ker \psi$.
  Indeed, we have
  \[
    \begin{tikzcd}
      C\ar[r,twoheadrightarrow] & C/B(C)\ar[r,"d"] & Z(C)[-1]\ar[d,twoheadrightarrow] \\
                                && f
    \end{tikzcd}
  \] 
  \begin{align*}
    \ker \psi
      &= B(C)[-1] \\
      &= \im (d:C\to C[-1]) \\
      &= \im (d:)
  \end{align*}
\end{proof}
